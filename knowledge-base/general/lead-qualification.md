# Lead Qualification

How the assistant figures out whether a prospect is a good fit and what to do next. Applies to the service lines (AI agency, website design, IT support/RMM, CRM). Hair store shoppers are customers, not leads, unless they ask about wholesale or bulk.

> Items in [BRACKETS] are for the owner to fill in.

## Principles
- Be helpful first. Answer their question, then ask.
- Ask **one question at a time**, conversationally. Never run a checklist.
- Stop qualifying once you know enough to route them.
- Never pressure. If they just want information, give it.

## What we want to learn
| Topic | Why it matters | Example question |
|---|---|---|
| Need | Picks the right service | "What are you hoping to get done?" |
| Business | Fit and context | "What kind of business is it, and roughly how many people?" |
| Timeline | Urgency | "When would you like this in place?" |
| Budget | Fit and scope | "Do you have a budget range in mind?" |
| Decision-maker | Who closes | "Will you be making the decision, or is anyone else involved?" |
| Current setup | Scope | "What are you using today?" |
| Contact | Follow-up | "What's the best email to reach you?" |

## Questions by service
- **AI agency:** What process or task do you want to automate? What tools and data do you use now? How many people or customers would it serve?
- **Website design:** New site or redesign? How many pages? Do you need e-commerce, bookings, or a blog? Do you have branding and content? Who handles updates after launch?
- **IT support / RMM:** How many users and devices? What systems and software? Any current IT provider? Any security or compliance needs? What's driving the change?
- **CRM:** Which CRM, if any, today? How many users? How are leads and customers tracked now? Do you need data migration?

## Scoring (internal, never shown to the prospect)
Give a rough score from what they've shared.

| Signal | Hot | Warm | Cold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Need | Clear, specific problem | Exploring options | Vague or curiosity |
| Timeline | within 30 days | 1–3 months | 3+ months or none |
| Budget | in line with our listed ranges | Unsure but open | far below our listed ranges / refuses |
| Authority | Decision-maker | Influencer | Researcher |

- **Hot:** need + timeline + budget fit + decision-maker. → Offer a call or booking right away, then escalate with a summary.
- **Warm:** interested but missing one or two signals. → Share relevant info, ask the missing question, offer next steps.
- **Cold:** vague, far off, or poor fit. → Be helpful, share a resource, invite them back.

## Minimums and fit
- Minimum project sizes: none are stated. Don't quote a minimum; explain the listed ranges honestly.
- Not a fit: requests outside our five service areas, anything needing legal or compliance advice, and budgets far below the listed ranges.
- If not a fit, say so kindly and, where possible, point to another option. Don't waste their time.

## Sample flow
> **Visitor:** I need a new website.
> **Assistant:** Happy to help. Is this a brand-new site or a redesign of an existing one?
> **Visitor:** Redesign. Ours is outdated.
> **Assistant:** Got it. Roughly how many pages, and do you need to sell online or take bookings?
> **Visitor:** About 10 pages, no store. We'd like to launch next month.
> **Assistant:** That's a realistic scope. Projects like that typically run €1,700–€5,000 plus VAT and take 3–6 weeks. Want to book a short call so we can confirm details? [no booking link yet: arrange a call by phone, WhatsApp, or email]

## Handling common situations
- **"How much does it cost?"** Give the documented range from the relevant folder, explain what drives cost, then ask a scoping question. Never invent a figure.
- **"Just browsing."** Answer, share a resource, leave the door open.
- **Wants multiple services.** Note each need, explain how they work together, and escalate as one lead.
- **Won't share details.** Don't push. Offer general info and a way to reach us.

## Data handling
- Collect only what's needed to follow up: name, email or phone, company, need.
- Never ask for passwords, payment details, or sensitive credentials.
- Tell people how their details will be used if they ask: there is no privacy policy link yet; say the team can explain how details are used and stored.
